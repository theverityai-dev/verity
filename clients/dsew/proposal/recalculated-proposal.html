The first proposal stated the AI adaptability inside the module prices as about ₹40,000 to ₹50,000 (Option 1) and ₹80,000 to ₹90,000 (Option 2). The amounts removed, ₹45,000 and ₹80,000 (₹75,000 plus ₹5,000), sit inside those stated ranges.

Why the AI came out

An adapter is a connection point, not a model: it does nothing until DSEW also buys hardware or a service behind it. Search and summaries over a 15-user installation are not on the path of any quotation, BOM, job, traveller or test. Carrying them in every module line made each line dearer and the server heavier, for a benefit DSEW has not asked to use on day one.

Why the automation came out

Escalation chains, cross-module triggers and auto-generated documents are tuned to a factory's habits after it has run the workflows for a while. Approval workflows and reminders stay, so the controlled chain is complete; the tuned automation can follow once DSEW's own testing shows which rules are worth having.

13Where things stand, and what follows

Verity's foundations (data isolation, permissions, audit, stock, manufacturing orders, QC checkpoints, project jobs, approvals, QR identity) exist and have been run on our test environments with fictional data. Revision-controlled BOMs, controlled documents, structured test records, HR and payroll, finance and the other modules beyond the foundations are built during the project, which is why the timelines are ranges.

Before DSEW goes live, these gates are passed together with DSEW (document 2, section 6): the compatibility check on DSEW's host and Windows Server 2019, a real-phone HTTPS upload, a rehearsed combined restore of database, files and identity, and the outage tests. Production use starts only after they pass.
