02What is in your workspace

Fabric rolls

Receive rolls by invoice, each with its own number and a printed QR label. Stock by fabric, a balance on every roll, a warning when a fabric runs low, a warning when the same supplier invoice is entered twice, and opening stock on launch day.

Lots, rate card and cutting

Plan a lot, set the rate per piece for each stage on the lot's own rate card (a stage can be skipped for a lot), and cut it from the chosen rolls in one step. Numbered bundles, B-0001 onward with no gaps, each with a slip: full size or a small sticker for the tie, printed together.

Give out and take back, by scan

Scan a bundle, pick the karigar, confirm the count, and a challan prints for the worker. Taking back is counted as good, rejected, rework and short. The receiver's count is the truth, and the supervisor who recorded each handover answers for it.

Cases and charges

Pieces that go short open a case on the worker who held them and raise a charge request for the lost pieces. Nothing is deducted silently: the manager or the owner decides within their limits, and the decision, who made it and why stay on the case.

Wages, advances and balances

The lot's rate times the accepted pieces. Advances, recoveries, deductions with a reason, payments with mode and reference, and an opening balance for what was owed before Verity. Every karigar has a clear account.

Lot costing

Fabric at the roll's invoice rate, wages, and the cost categories you choose (thread, printing, packing and more), less charges recovered. Cost per good piece for every lot, and a shared bill split by pieces cut. Two lots cut or finished together can be joined: one carries a part of the other's cost (all of it, a percentage or by pieces), the first lot's own records stay as they are, and the move can be reversed.

Team

Add a person in one sheet with their role and stages. Each karigar has a profile and a supervisor, so a supervisor's list shows their own people. Switch someone off when they leave; their history stays. People without an email sign in with a username.

Ready stock

The garments that finished the last stage, less what has gone out. Enter the stock already on the shelf as opening stock, take stock out as sold, dispatched or a sample, and correct a count with a reason. Every movement is on a ledger you can reverse and download.

Away, counted and printed

Mark a supervisor away and let another cover their stages and karigars for the dates. Count the fabric shelf by scanning roll numbers and post only the differences. The slip sheet remembers which bundles have a slip printed, and roll labels and slips also download as a file for a Zebra label printer.

Today

One page for the owner's morning or evening: what was given out, taken back, rejected, sent for rework and short on any day, what garments left, and how many requests wait for a decision. It reads the same entries as every report, so it can never disagree with them.

Routes that fit the factory

Mark a stage required or optional. A lot can skip only an optional stage, so a shirt without a collar and cuff follows its own route while every other lot keeps the full one. Each person opens on the page their work is on.

Reports and records

Reports led by graphics with the table folded below, each with Excel, CSV and Print or PDF. A change history of who did what and when. Defect photographs taken on the phone and attached to the entry.

Hindi and English: the floor screens, the daily entry, every slip, and the screens the munshi and the store use every day (fabric, receiving rolls, counting the shelf, ready stock, the karigar list) are bilingual, chosen on each phone. Lots, costs, reports and settings are in English today; the rest of the office is one of the next steps in section 05.
