02What is in your workspace

Plan a lot, cut numbered bundles (B-0001 onward, no gaps), and print a slip with the bundle number, net pieces, lot and a QR. Lots move through draft, active, on hold, completed and closed.

Issue, take back, rework

Give a bundle to a karigar or an outside unit with a due date. Take it back split into good, rejected, rework and short, with a live "accounted for 96 of 98" line. Rework goes back out and is counted on its own.

Slips, the munshi's daily entry and every supervisor screen in both languages, chosen on each phone and remembered. Dark and light themes for the cutting room and the evening shift.

05The supervisor screens

Supervisors work standing on the floor with a phone, so these screens are designed phone-first: one task per screen, large touch targets, counts entered with plus and minus controls, and no wage figures anywhere. On a desktop they use the same layout, wider.

S1 · My floor

Supervisor · phone and desktop
  • The stages this supervisor looks after, as chips, and today's date.
  • One ring gauge: pieces in my stages, split into out with karigars, waiting to be issued, rework and short.
  • "Late and due today": one card per open issue, showing the bundle, the karigar, pieces still out and the due date. Tapping a card opens S2 for that issue.
  • Two big buttons: Take back (S2) and Give out (S3).
  • Alerts for this supervisor's stages at the top, tappable.

S2 · Receive and inspect

Supervisor · phone first
  • Choose the issue coming back (cards grouped by karigar, each showing pieces out).
  • Enter good, rejected, rework and short with plus and minus steppers. A live line reads "accounted for 96 of 98, 2 will stay open"; it turns red if the total is more than the pieces out, and Save stays disabled.
  • Rejected and rework need a reason (stitch defect, measurement, stain, hole, other). Right after saving, a photograph can be taken with the phone camera and attached to the entry.
  • Saving writes one entry that cannot be edited later, only reversed with a reason (a supervisor reverses their own entry on the same day; the manager any entry). If rejected pieces would justify a wage deduction, the screen offers "Request deduction", which goes to the manager.

S3 · Give out

Supervisor · phone and desktop
  • Bundles waiting at my stage appear as cards with their pieces and the lot's cover photo.
  • Pick a karigar (the screen shows what they already hold and whether a rate is set), set the due date, and give out. The wage basis defaults to the karigar's usual and can be changed for one job.
  • Rework waiting in the pool is reissued from the same screen and carries its own "rework" flag.

S4 · Rework and shortage register

Supervisor, Manager
  • Every open rework piece by bundle and age, with the reason and photograph from when it came back. One tap reissues it.
  • Every open shortage (pieces that did not come back and cannot be found). The supervisor can request a write-off, which goes to the manager with the history. A piece that turns up later is corrected by the manager reversing the return and entering it again.
  • Nothing here changes a good count: rework is not output until it is redone and accepted.

S5 · Day close

Supervisor → Owner and Manager
  • A one-screen summary of the day for my stages: pieces given, taken back, good, rejected, rework, short, and what is late.
  • A short handover note in Hindi or English. Submitting it freezes the summary for the owner and manager to read.
  • Open warnings (a late return, an unreconciled shortage) are listed but do not block the close.

S6 · Karigar quick view

Supervisor
  • For a karigar: pieces held now, due dates, today's returns, and their reject and rework rate over the last 30 days. No rates, wages or balances.

Manager screens

M1 · Approvals

Manager, Owner
  • One inbox: deduction requests, shortage write-offs, lot hold and close. Each shows the facts (bundle, karigar, amount or pieces, the photograph, who asked) and two buttons, approve and decline with a reason.
  • Anything above the manager's limit is shown but only the owner can approve it.

M2 · Limits and assignments

Owner
  • Set the rupee limits for manager approvals, assign stages to each supervisor, and see each supervisor's day closes.

08What changes under the hood

For readers who want to know what "customization" means in practice. Detail is in document 5.

  • Roles. Four roles are created from permissions (an action on a record type within a scope). No role name is fixed in the code.
  • Stage assignment. A small record links a supervisor to the stages they look after. The piece-rate commands check it before accepting an issue or a return from a supervisor. The platform's own scope list is not changed.
  • Approvals. A request record (deduction, write-off, hold, close) with a state, an amount or quantity, the people involved and the decision. The request is kept inside the piece-rate capability, with the same company isolation as every other record; approving applies the ordinary change (a deduction entry, a lot status) through the same rules as any other user.
  • Photographs. A defect photograph is platform Evidence about the return entry, uploaded in two steps and checked before it is recorded, stored in the platform file store with the same company isolation as every other record. Lot pictures are set up with you.
  • Bilingual labels. A small label catalogue for the daily-entry and supervisor screens; the language is chosen on each device and remembered there.
  • Alerts. Late returns, open shortages and requests waiting for a decision are shown as alert cards on My floor and in Approvals. No SMS or WhatsApp in the MVP.

10How we agree it is done

  1. All four roles can sign in, and each sees only the screens in section 4. A supervisor cannot issue or receive at a stage that is not assigned to them, tested with a second supervisor.
  2. The reconciliation from your sheet reproduces exactly: 100 = 94 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 open.
  3. A return that accounts for more pieces than are out is refused with the figures; a correction is a reversal, and both rows stay.
  4. A rejected or rework entry accepts a photograph, and the photograph shows in the register and in the approval.
  5. A deduction or write-off above the manager's limit cannot be approved by the manager; the owner can.
  6. A closed lot cannot be changed.
  7. Every screen in this document opens at desktop width and at 390 px phone width, in both light and dark themes, with the demonstration data and no unrecorded defect.
  8. Hindi labels show on daily entry and every supervisor screen for a user whose language is Hindi.
  9. The demonstration reset command restores the data in under two minutes.
  10. A backup is taken and a restore is rehearsed on the Stylified workspace before go-live.

How it is built

For Stylified's owner and for anyone you ask to review the solution. It says what exists, what is built in this project, and where we are still working.

01Overview

Stylified gets its own workspace on Verity's hosted platform. The workspace is a set of screens and rules for the piece-rate cycle, on top of a shared foundation that provides sign-in, roles, an audit trail, files, notifications and company isolation.

Lots, bundles, stages, karigars and rates, issues and returns, rework, shortages, wages and money, approvals, day close, reports. Every change is a command with its own checks; every screen reads through a query.

Company isolation, identity and memberships, roles built from permissions, an audit trail, an event log, file storage, notifications, numbering, configuration.

03Company isolation

  • Every Stylified record carries the company it belongs to. The database itself refuses to return or accept a row for another company: row-level security is forced on each table and the company is set for each request from the signed-in user's verified membership, never from anything the browser sends.
  • If no company is set, a query returns nothing and a write is rejected: isolation fails closed.
  • The platform checks at startup that its database role is one that cannot bypass these rules, and refuses to run otherwise. An automated test proves that one company cannot read another's data.

04Roles and permissions

  • A permission is an action on a kind of record within a scope (for example "issue" on a piece-rate entry for the company). A role is a named set of permissions. There are no hard-coded role names in the code, so changing a role is a setting, not a release.
  • The four Stylified roles (Owner, Manager, Supervisor, Munshi) and their matrix are in document 2, section 4.
  • The server checks the permission for every command and every query. Hiding a button is only a convenience; it is never the control.
  • Supervisor stage scope. The platform's scope list stays as it is. A supervisor's assigned stages are held as a small record in the piece-rate module, and the issue and return commands refuse a supervisor acting on a stage that is not theirs. This is tested with a second supervisor.

How the figures are made

Nothing on a dashboard is typed in. Pieces out, pieces waiting, good, rejected, rework, short, days at a stage, wages and balances are all computed from the entries and payments by one piece of logic, which is covered by automated tests that use the worked examples from your requirement sheet (for example 100 pieces issued, 94 good, 2 rejected, 1 rework, 2 short and 1 still open; and ₹48,000 over 960 good pieces is ₹50 a piece). A figure on a report always agrees with the entries it is built from.

06Audit and corrections

  • Every command records who did it, when, from which role, and what changed, in an audit trail the owner can read.
  • Entries, payments and rate history cannot be updated or deleted by the application: a database rule rejects both. A wrong entry is cancelled by a reversal with a reason; the original and the reversal both stay visible.
  • A reversal that would leave a later stage with more pieces than it received is refused.

07Photographs

  • A photograph is uploaded from the phone camera or a file, checked for type and size, stored in the company's isolated storage, and shown through a short-lived link only to users who may see the record.
  • Pictures are resized for the screen so lists stay fast.

08Backups and recovery

  • A daily backup of the database and files, kept for at least 30 days, is part of the service (document 3, section 5). The mechanism (the hosting provider's backups plus our own nightly export) is configured and a restore is rehearsed on Stylified's workspace before go-live; that rehearsal is an acceptance criterion.
  • Because corrections are reversals, a restore is needed only for loss or damage, not for ordinary mistakes.

10Speed

We measured the local build: a page made about 10 to 16 database calls, mostly shared overhead from sign-in and navigation rather than the page itself. We have already removed the repeated identity lookups (the first of several steps), added an instant top progress bar and press feedback on every card and button, and replaced the lone loading spinner with a page-shaped skeleton. The remaining planned steps are folding the layout's queries into one, caching each user's navigation and permissions for about a minute, and narrowing the dashboard's queries as history grows. We will report measured times on Stylified's workspace before go-live, not estimates.
