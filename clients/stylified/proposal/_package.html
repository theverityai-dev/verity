02What is in your workspace

Fabric rolls

Receive rolls by invoice, each with its own number and a printed QR label. Stock by fabric, a balance on every roll, a warning when a fabric runs low, a warning when the same supplier invoice is entered twice, and opening stock on launch day.

Lots, rate card and cutting

Plan a lot, set the rate per piece for each stage on the lot's own rate card (a stage can be skipped for a lot), and cut it from the chosen rolls in one step. Numbered bundles, B-0001 onward with no gaps, each with a slip: full size or a small sticker for the tie, printed together.

Give out and take back, by scan

Scan a bundle, pick the karigar, confirm the count, and a challan prints for the worker. Taking back is counted as good, rejected, rework and short. The receiver's count is the truth, and the supervisor who recorded each handover answers for it.

Cases and charges

Pieces that go short open a case on the worker who held them and raise a charge request for the lost pieces. Nothing is deducted silently: the manager or the owner decides within their limits, and the decision, who made it and why stay on the case.

Wages, advances and balances

The lot's rate times the accepted pieces. Advances, recoveries, deductions with a reason, payments with mode and reference, and an opening balance for what was owed before Verity. Every karigar has a clear account.

Lot costing

Fabric at the roll's invoice rate, wages, and the cost categories you choose (thread, printing, packing and more), less charges recovered. Cost per good piece for every lot, and a shared bill split by pieces cut.

Team

Add a person in one sheet with their role and stages. Each karigar has a profile and a supervisor, so a supervisor's list shows their own people. Switch someone off when they leave; their history stays. People without an email sign in with a username.

Ready stock

The garments that finished the last stage, less what has gone out. Enter the stock already on the shelf as opening stock, take stock out as sold, dispatched or a sample, and correct a count with a reason. Every movement is on a ledger you can reverse and download.

Away, counted and printed

Mark a supervisor away and let another cover their stages and karigars for the dates. Count the fabric shelf by scanning roll numbers and post only the differences. The slip sheet remembers which bundles have a slip printed.

Routes that fit the factory

Mark a stage required or optional. A lot can skip only an optional stage, so a shirt without a collar and cuff follows its own route while every other lot keeps the full one. Each person opens on the page their work is on.

Reports and records

Reports led by graphics with the table folded below, each with Excel, CSV and Print or PDF. A change history of who did what and when. Defect photographs taken on the phone and attached to the entry.

Hindi and English: the floor screens, the daily entry and every slip are bilingual, chosen on each phone. The owner and office screens are in English today; a Hindi office interface is one of the next steps in section 05.

What we build, and for whom

The workspace runs a real lot from the fabric roll to the ready garment, with every handover counted by both sides, money and quality kept honest, and the cost of the lot known. 01The flow, in seven steps

#StepWhat happens
1Receive rollsEach roll gets a number and a printed QR label: fabric type, quantity, supplier, invoice. A repeated invoice is warned before it is saved
2Make a lotA design, a planned quantity, and a rate card: what each stage pays per piece. A stage can be skipped for a lot
3CutChoose the rolls, say how much fabric went in and how many pieces came out. Verity makes the numbered bundles, each with a slip and a QR

05The screens

Supervisors work standing on the floor with a phone, so their screens are phone-first: one task per screen, large touch targets, counts entered with plus and minus controls, and no wage figure anywhere. On a desktop they use the same layout, wider.

Scan

Supervisor, Store, Cutting master · phone
  • A viewfinder. Point the camera at a slip, a roll label or a challan, or type the number, or use a hand scanner.
  • A card for what was scanned, with its one next action for this person: give out, take back, or open the roll.

My floor

Supervisor · phone and desktop
  • The stages this supervisor looks after, and today's date. A gauge of pieces in these stages: out with karigars, waiting, rework, short.
  • "Late and due today": one card per open issue with the bundle, karigar, pieces still out and due date.
  • Alerts for these stages, tappable: late returns, open shortages, requests waiting.

Give out and take back

Supervisor · phone first
  • Give out: pick the karigar (from the supervisor's own list), confirm the count they accept, set a due date. The wage basis defaults to the karigar's and can be changed for one job. A challan prints.
  • Take back: enter good, rejected, rework and short with plus and minus. A live line reads "accounted for 96 of 98, 2 will stay open" and turns red if the total is more than is out; Save stays disabled.
  • Rejected and rework need a reason from your own list, and a photograph can be taken right after saving.
  • Saving writes one entry that cannot be edited, only reversed with a reason.

Cases

Supervisor, Manager, Owner
  • Every shortage as a case, oldest first: who held the pieces, how many, which bundle, who recorded it, what was decided, by whom and when.
  • A case opens a charge request for the lost pieces. It is decided by the manager or owner within their limits; a piece that turns up later is corrected by reversal.

Day close

Supervisor → Owner and Manager
  • A one-screen summary of the day for these stages: given, taken back, good, rejected, rework, short, and what is late.
  • A short handover note in Hindi or English. Submitting freezes the summary for the owner and manager to read.

08What changes under the hood

For readers who want to know what "customization" means in practice. Detail is in document 5.

  • Roles. Six roles are made from permissions (an action on a record type within a scope), installed once per workspace and editable. No role name is fixed in the code.
  • Stage and karigar scope. A small record links a supervisor to the stages they look after, and a karigar can be set under a supervisor. The commands that give out and take back check both, for every caller.
  • Rate card. A rate per stage for each lot, taken first; then the worker's own rate; then the stage's default. The rate used is copied onto each entry, so a later change never rewrites history.
  • Rolls and cost. A roll carries its invoice rate. Fabric used by a lot is recorded when it is cut, and the lot's cost is built from the records, never typed.
  • Cases and approvals. A shortage opens a case on the worker who held the pieces. Approving applies the ordinary change through the same rules as any other user.
  • Photographs. A defect photograph is platform evidence about the entry, uploaded in two steps and checked before it is recorded.
  • Bilingual labels. A label catalogue for the floor screens and daily entry; the language is chosen on each device and remembered there.

10How we agree it is done

Document 6 turns each of these into a step to run, the figures to expect and a box to tick, with the sign-off at the end.

  1. All six roles can sign in, and each sees only the screens in section 4. A supervisor cannot give out at a stage that is not assigned to them, nor to a karigar who is set under another supervisor, tested with a second supervisor.
  2. The reconciliation from your sheet reproduces exactly: 100 = 94 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 1 open.
  3. 100 sent and 98 received shows 2 as a case on the worker who held them, with a charge request, both people recorded and the handover history kept.
  4. A return that accounts for more pieces than are out is refused with the figures; a correction is a reversal, and both rows stay.
  5. A rejected or rework entry accepts a photograph, and the photograph shows in the register and in the approval.
  6. A deduction, charge or write-off above the manager's limit cannot be approved by the manager; the owner can.
  7. A closed lot cannot be changed.
  8. A roll is received with a label; scanning the label opens it; cutting a lot from rolls reduces their balances; a repeated invoice is warned.
  9. A lot's cost equals fabric plus wages plus other costs less charges recovered, and a cost that would be counted twice is refused.
  10. Opening fabric stock and a karigar's opening balance can be entered and reversed, and appear in the ledgers.
  11. A person added on the Team screen can sign in with the role and stages given; a person switched off cannot.
  12. Every report downloads to Excel and the totals equal the screen.
  13. Every screen in this document opens at desktop width and at 390 px phone width, in both light and dark themes, with your data and no unrecorded defect.
  14. Hindi labels show on the daily entry and every supervisor screen for a user whose language is Hindi.
  15. Ready stock: opening stock is entered, stock out beyond what is in stock is refused, a count correction needs a reason, and a reversal returns the stock with both rows kept.
  16. A lot can skip an optional stage but not a required one, and its pieces go on to the next stage.
  17. A backup is taken and a restore is rehearsed on the Stylified workspace before go-live.

01Overview

Stylified gets its own workspace on Verity's hosted platform. The workspace is a set of screens and rules for the piece-rate cycle, on top of a shared foundation that provides sign-in, roles, an audit trail, files, notifications and company isolation.

Experience

Web application for desktop, tablet and phone, dark and light themes. Supervisor screens are designed phone-first and open from a camera scan. Hindi and English labels on the floor screens, daily entry and slips.

Fabric rolls, lots and rate cards, bundles, stages, karigars and rates, handovers and returns, rework, cases, wages and money, lot costing, approvals, day close, team, reports. Every change is a command with its own checks; every screen reads through a query.

Platform foundation

Company isolation, identity and memberships, roles built from permissions, an audit trail, an event log, file storage, notifications, numbering, configuration.

Data and hosting

A relational database with row-level security forced on every Stylified table, file storage, application hosting in an Indian region, backups.

03Company isolation

  • Every Stylified record carries the company it belongs to. The database itself refuses to return or accept a row for another company: row-level security is forced on each table and the company is set for each request from the signed-in user's verified membership, never from anything the browser sends.
  • If no company is set, a query returns nothing and a write is rejected: isolation fails closed.
  • The platform checks at startup that its database role is one that cannot bypass these rules, and refuses to run otherwise. An automated test proves that one company cannot read another's data.

04Roles and permissions

  • A permission is an action on a kind of record within a scope (for example "issue" on a piece-rate entry for the company). A role is a named set of permissions. There are no hard-coded role names in the code, so changing a role is a setting, not a release.
  • The six Stylified roles (Owner, Manager, Supervisor, Munshi, Store, Cutting master) and their matrix are in document 2, section 4. They are installed once per workspace from a preset and can be edited.
  • The server checks the permission for every command and every query. Hiding a button is only a convenience; it is never the control.
  • Supervisor scope. The platform's scope list stays as it is. A supervisor's assigned stages are held as a small record in the piece-rate module, and a karigar can be set under a supervisor. The give-out and take-back commands refuse a supervisor acting on a stage that is not theirs, and a give-out to a karigar who is set under another supervisor. This is tested with a second supervisor.

How the figures are made

Nothing on a dashboard is typed in. Pieces out, pieces waiting, good, rejected, rework, short, days at a stage, roll balances, wages, balances and lot costs are all computed from the entries, movements and payments by one piece of logic, which is covered by automated tests that use the worked examples from your requirement sheet (for example 100 pieces issued, 94 good, 2 rejected, 1 rework, 2 short and 1 still open; and ₹48,000 over 960 good pieces is ₹50 a piece). A figure on a report always agrees with the entries it is built from.

06Audit and corrections

  • Every command records who did it, when, from which role, and what changed, in an audit trail the owner can read.
  • Entries, payments and rate history cannot be updated or deleted by the application: a database rule rejects both. A wrong entry is cancelled by a reversal with a reason; the original and the reversal both stay visible.
  • A reversal that would leave a later stage with more pieces than it received is refused.

07Photographs

  • A photograph is uploaded from the phone camera or a file, checked for type and size, stored in the company's isolated storage, and shown through a short-lived link only to users who may see the record.
  • Pictures are resized for the screen so lists stay fast.

08Backups and recovery

  • A daily backup of the database and files, kept for at least 30 days, is part of the service (document 3, section 5). The mechanism (the hosting provider's backups plus our own nightly export) is configured and a restore is rehearsed on Stylified's workspace before go-live; that rehearsal is an acceptance criterion.
  • Because corrections are reversals, a restore is needed only for loss or damage, not for ordinary mistakes.
