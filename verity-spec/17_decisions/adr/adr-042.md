# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-042: Customer self-order (a guest orders from a table QR, a pickup QR or a kiosk)

* **Decision ID**: ADR-042
* **Status**: ACCEPTED (2026-10-09) by the product owner ("yes go ahead complete it all", in reply to the plan and the four ADRs). Build authorised; progress is in taskplan 126. **A new
  externally reachable write surface: `CLAUDE.md` stop conditions require this ADR before any code.**
* **Question**: URY lets a guest scan a table QR, browse the menu, add to the running order, call a
  waiter or ask for the bill, and offers pickup QR, kiosk and table-tablet modes. Verity has the channel
  labels `qr` and `website` and no guest-facing surface. How may an unauthenticated person place an order
  without a second authority path, without weakening INV-001, and without opening the system to pranks?
* **Context**:
  * URY evidence: `ury/ury/api/self_ordering.py` (signed QR token per table, enrolled devices with a
    hashed credential, sessions with idle timeout, service requests), `doctype/ury_self_ordering_profile`,
    `ury_ordering_session`, `ury_service_request`. Its QR signing secret is one value per profile.
  * Precedents that already bind this design: **ADR-017** (every action runs as a real human or
    provisioned `ActorContext`; `channel` is provenance only), **ADR-029** (a machine caller is a
    provisioned identity behind a revocable, hashed credential; tenant comes from the credential; no
    destructive commands; idempotency key; dark by default), **ADR-030** (an unauthenticated route
    reaches data only through one `SECURITY DEFINER` function keyed by a hashed unguessable token),
    **ADR-001 and ADR-007** (a walk-in is not a Party; no identity is minted for a guest),
    **PLA-TEN-006** (tenant is never taken from a request payload).
  * The risk specific to ordering: a table sticker is a long-lived secret printed on a wall. Anyone who
    photographs it could order from home against a table that is empty.
* **Decision**: a guest is not an identity. A guest holds a **session token** that is a narrow,
  expiring credential for one provisioned ordering identity at one outlet, and what the guest submits is
  a *proposal* that staff accept unless the table is already seated by staff.
* **Constraints**:
  1. **No Party, no User per guest.** The session is a tenant-scoped row (`self_order_session`) bound to
     one outlet and one of: a table, a pickup point, a kiosk. It stores only a SHA-256 hash of its token.
  2. **Two kinds of token.** A **table link** printed on the sticker is a stable random value stored
     hashed; opening it creates a *session* only if the table is currently seated by staff, and the
     session gets its own fresh token held in the URL path (no cookie). The sticker alone never orders.
     A **pickup link** opens a session at any time (that is its purpose) and is rate limited harder.
  3. **Sessions expire and close.** Idle timeout 30 minutes, absolute 3 hours, closed when the table's
     bill is settled or the table is released. A closed or expired token answers identically to a wrong
     one.
  4. **One ordering identity per outlet, provisioned the ordinary way.** An administrator provisions a
     User "Self order, <outlet>" with `provisionIdentity()` (ADR-007) and a Role `Self Order Guest` that
     holds exactly: Read on menu category, item, variant, modifier and availability; Create on a
     self-order submission; Create on a service request. Nothing else. The route resolves the session
     (one definer function, `verity.self_order_lookup(token_hash)`) to tenant, outlet, table, session id
     and that identity, then calls `executeCommand` as its `ActorContext` with channel `api` and the
     session id in the audit metadata. `enforcePolicy()` decides, as for every caller. There is no
     service account and no carve-out in `policy.ts`.
  5. **Guests propose; staff or a seated table accept.** A submission (`self_order_submission` with
     snapshot lines) is *pending* until a staff member accepts it at the order pad, which runs
     `add_order_lines` **as that human** (the line's `takenByUserId` is the accepter). **Exception, one
     rule:** when staff have already seated the table and opened its order, a guest's lines are applied
     directly as the ordering identity, because a human has already vouched for the table. A staff member
     can reject a pending submission with a reason; the guest sees "not accepted, please ask a waiter".
  6. **The server prices everything.** A submission carries item ids, portion, add-on ids, quantity and
     note. Price, availability (outlet, channel, time window), modifiers and tax come from the same
     functions the order pad uses (including ADR-043's resolver); an unavailable item is refused with the
     same reason the pad shows. Quantity and line count are capped per submission and per session.
  7. **The guest sees only its own session.** The public read is a minimal projection: menu (names,
     descriptions, prices, add-ons, availability), the session's own lines with a derived status label
     (received, being prepared, ready, served), and a bill-request button. No other table, no staff
     names, no totals of other orders, no customer data. Optional name and phone for pickup are stored
     on the submission only and shown to staff, with a consent line on the page.
  8. **Service requests.** `service_request` (outlet, table, kind `waiter | bill`, status `open |
     acknowledged | resolved`, session id). Staff see an open request as a badge on the floor plan and
     resolve it; with ADR-039's in-app channel it also raises a notification. Requesting the bill does
     not generate it.
  9. **No online payment.** Pay at the counter only. A payment gateway, payment links and card terminals
     are a separate ADR (credentials and `GOV-SCO-006`).
  10. **Abuse limits.** Per-source and per-session throttling through the shared limiter; a cap on
      open pending submissions per session and per outlet (outlet setting); `Cache-Control: no-store`,
      `robots: noindex`; the public routes accept GET (read) and one POST (submit or request) only, with
      an `Idempotency-Key` so a retry does not double-submit.
  11. **Dark by default, per outlet.** `outlet_profile.self_order_enabled` is false until the owner turns
      it on; when false every public route answers 404 and no session can be created. Turning it off
      closes open sessions.
  12. **Retention.** Closed sessions, submissions and requests are purged after 30 days by the scheduled
      job (ADR-015); the audit trail of accepted orders is untouched.
* **Rejected**:
  * A single shared signing secret per profile (URY): one leak reaches every table; random hashed tokens
    have no shared secret.
  * An anonymous database role or an RLS exception for the public route (ADR-019, ADR-030): a second
    tenant-access surface.
  * Tenant or outlet taken from the URL path or payload (PLA-TEN-006).
  * Auto-placing every guest order into the kitchen: a prank or a typo reaches the pass with no human
    in the loop. The seated-table exception keeps the fast path where a human has vouched.
  * A Party or Customer record per guest (ADR-001, ADR-007).
  * Staff PIN to assign a tablet to a table (URY): a second credential path outside ADR-020 and already
    declined for attendance (DECISIONS.md #2). A kiosk or tablet here is a staff-opened session, not an
    enrolled device.
* **Consequences**: tables `self_order_session`, `self_order_submission` (+ lines), `service_request`,
  the definer function, two public routes under a path that cannot collide with the shell, a role and a
  provisioning step in outlet setup, a Settings toggle, a floor badge, a pending-submission queue on the
  pad, tests against the real database as an anonymous visitor (as ADR-030 did), and a printed-sticker
  screen. The taskplan puts this last: it depends on ADR-039's in-app channel, ADR-040's outlet profile
  and ADR-041's stations.
* **Owner**: Product Owner
* **Supersedes**: nothing. Applies ADR-029 and ADR-030 to a write surface.
* **Open for the approver**: (a) the caps (proposed: 3 pending per session, 30 lines per submission,
  10 pending per outlet); (b) whether pickup needs a phone number to submit (proposed: yes).
