# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-040: A dine-in bill is a GST tax invoice (numbered series, per-line rates, credit notes)

* **Decision ID**: ADR-040
* **Status**: PROPOSED (2026-10-09), awaiting product-owner approval. Nothing is built.
* **Question**: A dine-in `Bill` today is identified on screen by the first eight characters of its
  UUID (`BillView.tsx`), carries one tenant-wide CGST and SGST rate pair for every line, has no seller
  identity (GSTIN, FSSAI, legal name), no service charge, and a refund produces no credit note. An
  Indian restaurant bill is a tax invoice. What must it carry, and where does the number come from?
* **Context**:
  * Found while comparing URY with Verity (`docs/reference/ury-parity/ury-vs-verity-a-to-z.md`).
    Colonel Kebabz has been billing on this model since 2026-09-10.
  * The trading capability already solves numbering: `nextDocumentNumber` allocates a gapless,
    per-series, per-financial-year number under an advisory lock inside the caller's transaction, and
    invoices snapshot the seller's identity (`trading_invoice_series`, `TradingInvoice.sellerGstinSnapshot`).
    The allocator is welded to the trading registration check (`assertRegularRegistration`), so a
    restaurant tenant that never activates trading cannot call it.
  * Precedent: Colonel Kebabz DECISIONS.md #1 chose capability-native over generalising a live flow,
    keeping field names identical so a later merge is a rename. ADR-018 says extract a shared piece when
    a second client needs it; the dine-in bill is that second consumer for the allocator only.
  * CGST Rule 46 asks for a consecutive serial number, unique for the financial year, of at most 16
    characters. The 16-character limit must be confirmed with the client's tax adviser before cutover;
    this ADR designs to it so the question cannot bite later.
* **Decision**:
  1. **A bill gets its number when it is generated, from a gapless series per outlet per financial
     year.** The allocator is split: the pure sequence allocation moves to
     `src/server/runtime/document-number.ts` (no registration check); trading's `nextDocumentNumber`
     becomes "assert registration, then allocate", behaviour unchanged. Dine-in calls the allocator
     directly. The series rows stay in `trading_invoice_series` (a table rename is cosmetic, touches
     live data paths and is deferred). Series keys are distinct (`BILL-<outlet code>`), so nothing
     collides with trading.
  2. **Display format is composed by the capability, not the allocator**: `<outlet code>/<yy-yy>/<6
     digits>`, for example `DC/26-27/000123` (15 characters). The outlet code is at most three
     characters. A rolled-back transaction returns the number; a generated bill is never deleted and a
     bill generated in error keeps its number and is marked void.
  3. **Outlet identity is its own record**, `outlet_profile` (one per `Location`, dine-in owned): outlet
     code (unique per tenant), legal name, GSTIN, state code, registration type (`regular` or
     `composition`; an unregistered outlet issues a bill of supply with no tax columns), FSSAI number,
     address lines, business-day start (consequence 6), service-charge rate. The bill **snapshots**
     legal name, GSTIN, FSSAI and address at generation, as trading invoices do, so a later edit never
     restates a bill a guest holds.
  4. **Tax is per line, not per bill.** `MenuItem` gains an optional `taxRateBp` (basis points); null
     means the tenant default (`verity.dinein.tax.cgst_rate` plus `sgst_rate`, which stay). An order line
     snapshots its rate. The bill stores one `bill_tax_line` row per distinct rate (taxable value, CGST,
     SGST). The existing `cgst_minor`, `sgst_minor` and `taxable_minor` columns on `bill` stay as the
     totals so every current reader keeps working.
  5. **Intra-state only.** Restaurant service is supplied at the outlet, so CGST plus SGST applies.
     IGST is not built; an order the system would have to tax inter-state is refused with a message
     (trigger to revisit: a client with inter-state catering or corporate supply).
  6. **Platform orders are tax-free on the restaurant's own invoice by default.** For a channel in
     `delivery_platform` the e-commerce operator collects and pays the tax on restaurant services
     (Section 9(5) CGST Act). The outlet profile carries a per-channel switch, default "no tax on the
     restaurant's invoice for `delivery_platform`", editable by the owner. This replaces URY's "create
     invoice without tax" branch flag with a stated reason. Confirm with the client's tax adviser.
  7. **Service charge is optional, voluntary and taxed with the supply.** Off by default. When on, the
     outlet rate is added as its own line, apportioned across the bill's tax rates in proportion to
     taxable value so that tax follows the supply; a manager may waive it on one bill
     (`ActionExecute`, audited). Tips remain out of scope.
  8. **A refund is a credit note.** `bill_refund` gains a credit-note number (separate series
     `CN-<outlet code>`), the tax reversed per rate (proportional to the refunded lines or amount, same
     rounding rule as the existing by-items refund), and the original bill number. Refunds stay
     append-only (INV-002).
  9. **Cutover without rewriting history.** Bills generated before the cutover keep a null number and
     render their legacy label ("Bill AB12CD34"); the cutover date is recorded on the outlet profile.
     Whether pre-cutover bills need legal numbers is for the client's tax adviser and is not decided
     here.
* **Constraints**:
  * Money stays integer minor units; rates stay basis points; totals are computed once on the server
    and stored. A reprint a year later shows the rate that applied (existing rule, kept).
  * Allocation happens inside the generate-bill transaction. Two tills generating at once serialise per
    series; at restaurant volume that is invisible.
  * INV-001, ADR-017 and `enforcePolicy()` are untouched. New entities (`outlet_profile`,
    `bill_tax_line`) are tenant-scoped with forced RLS and added to the conformance lists; credit-note
    rows are append-only.
  * Tax screens (`/tax/*`) read dine-in bills as B2C by rate and state, and credit notes, in a later
    slice of the same plan; this ADR does not change them.
* **Rejected**:
  * Using the order id or a UUID fragment as the bill number (not consecutive, not legal).
  * Extracting GST registration and business profile out of trading into a shared capability now: a
    migration of live plywood data for a cosmetic gain. Revisit at a third consumer (rule of three).
  * Calling trading's `nextDocumentNumber` from dine-in unchanged: it throws for any tenant without a
    trading registration and would couple two capabilities through a registration check.
  * Per-bill tax rate chosen at the counter: lets a cashier choose the tax.
  * Daily-reset bill numbers: a tax invoice series is per financial year.
* **Consequences**: one migration (`outlet_profile`, `bill_tax_line`, columns on `menu_item`,
  `order_line`, `bill`, `bill_refund`); allocator extraction with trading tests unchanged; Settings screen
  for the outlet profile; Menu tax-rate column; bill and refund templates change; reports that sum tax read
  `bill_tax_line`. **Business day**: the business-day start lives on the outlet profile because the
  sales summary, dashboard, cash reconciliation and P&L all need one answer to "what day is it"; the
  hard-coded five-hour overhang in `serviceDayRange` is replaced by it.
* **Owner**: Product Owner
* **Supersedes**: nothing. Extends ADR-018's extraction rule; does not change ADR-001, ADR-005 or
  Colonel Kebabz DECISIONS.md #1.
* **Open for the approver**: (a) confirm the 16-character rule and the Section 9(5) default with the
  client's tax adviser; (b) pre-cutover bills (item 9); (c) whether `composition` outlets are needed in
  the first build or only `regular`.
