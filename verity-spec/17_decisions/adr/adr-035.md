# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-035: The Verity operator has full authority inside a client they have entered

* **Decision ID**: ADR-035
* **Status**: ACCEPTED (2026-10-06) on the product owner's explicit instruction ("the verity admin
  must have super access of everything for every client").
* **Amends**: ADR-013 (Global HQ Operator Security Model), the clause in `operator.ts` that kept the
  operator's in-client role "deliberately narrow". Does not change INV-001, ADR-017, ADR-034 or
  `enforcePolicy()`.
* **Question**: When Verity support enters a client, should the support role administer only
  identity, organization, roles and modules, or everything the client's own owner can do?
* **Context**: With the narrow role, an operator who entered a client to set it up or to diagnose a
  problem could not open the client's business screens (inventory, billing, orders) and could not
  create the client's first logins for a test client that has no users yet. Verity is run by its
  founders for 10–100 person businesses, and support routinely needs to do exactly what the owner
  can do.

* **Decision**:
  1. The `Verity Operator` role inside a client holds every verb (`Read`, `Create`, `Edit`,
     `Delete`, `ActionExecute`) on every entity registered on the platform (`entity_definition`),
     plus every entity any role in that client already uses, plus the platform entities in
     `OPERATOR_GRANTS`. Scope is `Tenant`.
  2. It is reconciled on every entry and every HQ action, so a capability installed later is
     covered the next time an operator enters.
  3. Nothing about how authority is obtained changes. The operator still has no access to any
     client they have not entered. Entering still needs platform operator authority, a stated
     reason recorded in the client's own security trail, and shows the support banner (ADR-034).
     Every action still passes `enforcePolicy()` as an ordinary membership and is audited with the
     operator's user id, which the platform audit marks as an operator action.

* **What this is not**: not a `Global` scope grant, not an RLS bypass, not a cross-tenant read. The
  operator acts inside one client at a time, through that client's own row-level security.

* **Consequences**: an operator can do anything the client's owner can, including refunds,
  approvals and creating logins. Because the role name is visible in the client's Roles list, a
  client administrator could assign it to their own staff; that grants nothing a client
  administrator could not already grant with a role of their own. Revisit (narrow per job, or add
  a time limit) if the operator team grows beyond the founders.
