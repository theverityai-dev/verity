# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-050: CRM companies and people are tenant-owned records, not Party rows

* **Decision ID**: ADR-050
* **Status**: PROPOSED (2026-10-20). Written for Task 128 (CRM capability) on the product owner's instruction the same day
  ("go ahead complete it all"). Built on the local development database only; not accepted until the owner reviews it.
* **Question**: INV-003 says one Party record per person or business, and `Party` models "human or corporate participant". Should
  the companies and contacts a client keeps in its CRM be `Party` rows, as Task 128's first draft (decision D1) proposed?
* **Context** (read 2026-10-20):
  * `party` is a **global** table (`GLOBAL_MODELS` in `conformance.test.ts`: "global identity — Bible V2 Primitive 2 §2, INV-003").
    It has no `tenant_id`.
  * Its row-level security makes a Party visible to a tenant only through a user who holds a membership in that tenant:
    `verity.party_visible()` (`20260823010000_identity_membership`) checks `"user" JOIN tenant_membership` for the current tenant.
    A contact who never signs in has no user and no membership, so a CRM contact written as a Party would be invisible to the very
    tenant that wrote it.
  * Making it visible means widening `party_visible()` to "any Party a CRM row in this tenant points at". Then two tenants that both
    keep the same person point at one global row, and one tenant's edit to that person's name, email or phone changes what the other
    tenant sees. That is a cross-tenant write path through a shared row: an INV-001 violation, not a convenience.
  * The code already keeps business counterparties off Party: `TradingCustomer` (trading), the guest `Customer` (crm, ADR-001/007
    reasoning), and `HrEmployee` points at Party only because employees are, or become, users.
  * ADR-007 already settles identity linking: identity is linked when a person verifies a contact channel. A CRM contact is
    information a tenant holds *about* someone, not that person's identity.
* **Decision**:
  1. A CRM **company** and **person** are tenant-scoped rows owned by the `relationship` capability (`relationship_company`,
     `relationship_person`), with forced RLS like every other tenant table. They are not `Party` rows and do not reference one.
  2. INV-003 is read as governing **identity** (who can act, who a user is): one Party per real person or business that has, or is
     being invited to, an identity in Verity. It does not require every tenant's address book to be a global identity table. The
     guest CRM, trading customers and now CRM contacts all follow this reading.
  3. If a CRM contact is later invited to sign in (a customer portal), the invitation creates or links a `Party` through the
     existing `provisionIdentity()` / ADR-007 path, and the CRM row records the link (`party_id`, nullable). That column is not
     added now; it is added with the first feature that needs it.
  4. Linking a CRM company to a trading customer (DSEW: the company a quote and job are for) is a keyed reference owned by the
     capability that needs it (`project` when it opens a job from a won opportunity), so `relationship` depends on nothing.
* **Consequences**:
  * No platform RLS function changes. No global table gains tenant data.
  * One real business can exist as a CRM company in several tenants with different details; that is correct, because each tenant
    owns its own view of its counterparties.
  * Task 128 decision D1 is superseded by this ADR.
* **Alternatives rejected**: Party-backed contacts with a widened `party_visible()` (cross-tenant write path through a shared row);
  a per-tenant shadow of Party (a second identity table, which is exactly what INV-003 forbids for identities).
