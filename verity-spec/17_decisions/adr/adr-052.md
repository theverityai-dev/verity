# Architectural Decision Record

## ADR-052: Email and calendar sync for the CRM — a new inbound security boundary (PROPOSED, not built)

* **Decision ID**: ADR-052
* **Status**: PROPOSED (2026-10-21). Written for Task 128 stage 5. **Nothing is built.** `CLAUDE.md` stop conditions require a
  decision before code whenever a security boundary is added; this is that decision, for the product owner to accept, amend or
  reject. A client must also want it: DSEW keeps confidential data on its own server, and whether its mail may be read by
  Verity is DSEW's decision.
* **Question**: Twenty attaches a person's email threads and calendar meetings to companies and people automatically. Should
  Verity's CRM do the same, and if so, under what rules?
* **What it adds that Verity does not have today**:
  1. **Stored third-party credentials**: an OAuth refresh token per user for Google or Microsoft 365. Verity stores no
     per-user third-party token anywhere today. MET-AUT-003 requires an encrypted credential registry, and where its key
     lives is already an open implementation decision in `CLAUDE.md`.
  2. **Inbound personal data at volume**: message headers and bodies of people who never used Verity, arriving on a
     schedule rather than through a person's command.
  3. **An outbound network dependency** from the scheduler to Google or Microsoft APIs (on-premise installs may not allow it).
* **Proposed decision** (to accept or amend):
  1. **Per-user, opt-in, revocable.** A person connects their own mailbox from their own account page; nobody connects
     another person's. Disconnecting deletes the token and stops the sync; already-captured records stay, marked with source.
  2. **Headers by default, bodies by choice.** Capture sender, recipients, subject, time and thread id, and match
     participants to CRM people by email (exact match only, never creating people silently). Message bodies are stored only
     when the user opts in, and never for messages where no participant is a CRM person.
  3. **Blocklist first**: personal domains, internal domains and any address or domain the user excludes are never captured.
  4. **Visibility follows the CRM record and the connecting user**: a captured email is a new interaction kind `email_sync`,
     readable by anyone who can read the person it attaches to, unless the user marks the connection private, in which case
     only they see bodies and others see "an email was exchanged" with the date.
  5. **Tokens** live in the credential registry (MET-AUT-003), encrypted with a key supplied from the deployment environment,
     never in the database in clear, never in logs (`telemetry-scrub.ts` rules apply). Scopes are read-only
     (`gmail.metadata` / `Mail.ReadBasic`, and full read only when bodies are enabled).
  6. **Runs as a platform schedule per connected user** under that user's own authority (ADR-017 reasoning: no service
     account with wider reach); failures are recorded through `command_failure`, never retried in a tight loop.
  7. **On-premise (ADR-031 Mode A)**: off unless the deployment sets the provider credentials; the screen says why.
* **Not decided here**: sending email from Verity (a separate outbound-communication decision), call recording (out of
  scope for Task 128), calendar write-back.
* **Consequences if accepted**: one new platform table for connected accounts, one for captured message headers, a schedule,
  and an account settings screen; a new interaction kind in the CRM. Estimated 5-8 days with Claude, after acceptance.
* **Alternatives**: forwarding address (users BCC a per-tenant address; no stored tokens, but needs inbound mail
  infrastructure); manual logging only (what is built today: the CRM's Email interaction kind).
